Thank you for the opportunity to revise "AI researchers as the next coal: transition rates, minimum viable research communities and the preservation of variety in global AI/ML". I resubmit it under the revised title "Measuring research-community dynamics from bibliometric career transitions: evidence from global AI/ML", which states the contribution more accurately for an informetrics readership. This letter answers your three requests point by point; a fuller point-by-point document is attached alongside.

1. Length. The manuscript now totals approximately 9,603 words counting main text, figure captions, table contents and references - down from 11,338 (~15% reduction). Adding the text drawn inside the figures themselves (about 195 words extracted from the figure-generation source, conservatively counted) keeps the total below 10,000. I compressed duplicated background and re-enumerated results, condensed the historical-analogy passage into a single evidence-carrying sentence, removed the Jevons framing, and moved the measurement-sensitivity summary and the policy-instrument table to Supplementary Material (Supplementary Tables S13-S14) while keeping the aim, operational definitions, model equations, calibration, all main results, the origin/PI measurement limits, the region-specific-M sensitivity finding, the structural-invariance explanation, the M non-identifiability result, the predictive-validity limits and the conclusions in the main text, per your guidance.

2. Engagement with informetrics literature. The revision engages the mobility-measurement and career-science literature substantively rather than by citation count. The Introduction now situates the work inside affiliation-based migration measurement (Moed et al., Scientometrics 2013; Robinson-García et al., Journal of Informetrics 2019), author-level mobility-productivity indicators (Sugimoto et al., Nature 2017; Halevi et al. 2016; Zhao et al., Journal of Informetrics 2020; Liu et al., Journal of Informetrics 2022; Jonkers & Cruz-Castro, Scientometrics 2008), return migration, knowledge transfer (Aman, Quantitative Science Studies 2020) and career science (Petersen et al., PNAS 2012/2014/2015; Wang et al., Nature Communications 2019). The measurement-ambiguity paragraph now grounds disambiguation, multi-affiliation and authorship-order uncertainty in Tekles & Bornmann (QSS 2020), D'Angelo & van Eck (Scientometrics 2020), Macháček et al. (Science and Public Policy 2022), Costas et al. (Scientometrics 2011) and Sauermann & Haeussler (Science Advances 2017). A new Discussion subsection, 'Comparison with existing informetric research', states precisely what the framework adds over those indicators - a dynamical state variable propagated from the same transition measurements, plus a quantified account of how sensitive community-level rankings are to the operational definitions. Citations are distributed across Introduction, Methods and Discussion rather than clustered in the Introduction.

3. Clarity and readability. The manuscript was restructured for an informetrics audience: the Introduction follows mobility-as-informetric-subject, measurement ambiguity, the individual-to-community gap, the methodological contribution, the case choice, and the research questions; the Discussion now runs principal findings, comparison with existing informetric research, methodological contribution, implications for research evaluation, conditional policy implications, limitations and future validation. Terminology was unified (research macro-region, active pool, minimum viable coauthor scale, model-implied viability boundary, boundary proximity), sentences and paragraphs were shortened, and the text is in the first person singular throughout.

On substance, the paper is unchanged: it reports a reproducible measurement pipeline - career-transition rates, a calibrated six-compartment model, and the T/M and boundary-proximity diagnostic pair - calibrated on 623,176 OpenAlex AI/ML authors across 9 research macro-regions, with every operational definition stress-tested in the main results. Dropout, not outflow, is the most elastic lever in 9 of 9 macro-regions, and the T/M ranking's dependence on the operational M is quantified, not asserted away. The manuscript claims no validated predictive power; it is a monitoring diagnostic.

All results are reproducible from the public repository named in the Data and Code Availability statement; a single script regenerates every number, figure and table.
