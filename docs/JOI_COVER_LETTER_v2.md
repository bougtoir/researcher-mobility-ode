Dear Editors of the Journal of Informetrics,

I submit the manuscript "AI researchers as the next coal: transition rates, minimum viable research communities and the preservation of variety in global AI/ML" for consideration as a Full Length Article.

Why it matters. Researcher-mobility measurement today reports stocks and net flows, which describe redistribution but cannot tell an evaluator whether a research system can still reproduce its own workforce. What the field lacks is a community-level state variable that tracks, in model terms, whether a system retains the capacity to reproduce its workforce before the headcount shows it. This paper supplies that variable and the complete, auditable measurement pipeline behind it.

What is new. The pipeline turns open bibliometric records into cohort career-transition rates, a calibrated six-compartment dynamical model, and a monitoring indicator pair (the equilibrium active pool relative to a minimum viable coauthor scale, T/M, and the proximity of that equilibrium to a model-implied viability boundary); the two are linked by construction and form one diagnostic scale, not two independent tests, and predictive validation is left as future work. Applied to 623,176 OpenAlex AI/ML authors (2000-2023) across 9 research macro-regions, it shows that distance to the boundary is governed by relative scale, not size: the lowest T/M is 215.76 (Anglosphere ex-US), and the closest boundary is in Anglosphere ex-US, where exogenous entry rate (I_0) reduced to 0.5% of its current value-a 99.5% reduction-would drive the pool to its threshold. Dropout, not outflow, is the most elastic lever in 9 of 9 macro-regions.

Why methodological contribution comes first. Every measurement choice in the pipeline is stress-tested and reported in the main results rather than in footnotes: origin assignment under seven specifications (no macro-region reverses mobility direction), PI status under six alternative definitions propagated through the model (T-side rankings are structurally invariant, P-side quantities vary and are reported), multi-affiliation prevalence and missingness/censoring bounds are quantified. The pipeline is field-agnostic by construction-AI/ML is the case study, not the object of the paper-which is why I believe it fits the Journal of Informetrics' interest in reusable measurement and evaluation methodology.

All results are reproducible from the public repository named in the Data and Code Availability statement; a single script regenerates every number, figure and table.
